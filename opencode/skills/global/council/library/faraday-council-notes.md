# The Greatest Minds Council: Michael Faraday

**Domain:** Experimental Science, Self-Education, Visualization, and Electric Motor Discovery
**Primary Sources:** Royal Institution Lab Notebooks (1821–1831), *The Chemical History of a Candle*, David Ricketts RI Lectures.

---

## 1. Deep Historical Context & Breakthroughs
- **The 1821 Electric Motor Breakthrough:** On September 3, 1821, after studying Oersted’s discovery that current-carrying wires deflect magnetic needles, Faraday realized the force was circular rather than linear. Using a simple apparatus of a vertical wire dipping into mercury around a magnet, he produced continuous circular rotation—inventing the first electric motor.
- **1831 Electromagnetic Induction:** Ten years later, he proved that changing magnetic fields induce electrical current, laying the foundation for all modern electrical generation.
- **The 291-Page Gift:** As an apprentice bookbinder, Faraday attended Sir Humphry Davy’s lectures, took exhaustive notes, illustrated them by hand, bound them into a 291-page book with a custom index, and presented it to Davy. This wasn't just a portfolio; it was absolute proof of disciplined execution and hunger for knowledge.

## 2. Core Mental Models & Working Habits
- **Action Before Abstraction:** Never get trapped in pure mathematical theory before testing physical phenomena in the lab. Build simple physical models immediately.
- **Anomaly Hunting:** When an experiment yields an unexpected result (like the subtle attraction/rejection curves at the ends of needles), treat it as a clue to a deeper law rather than experimental error.
- **Plain Language Translation:** Believed that if a scientific concept cannot be explained simply (as demonstrated in his famous Christmas lectures on a candle), it is not fully understood.

## 3. Imitate / Adapt / Avoid
- **Imitate:** Meticulous, dated laboratory and project notebooks; starting hard problems with simple physical prototypes.
- **Adapt:** Translating self-directed engineering study into rigorous, demonstrable deliverables.
- **Avoid:** Waiting for formal credentials before tackling complex technical problems.
