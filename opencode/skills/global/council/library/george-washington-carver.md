# The Greatest Minds Council: George Washington Carver

**Domain:** Agricultural Science, Sustainable Innovation, Resourcefulness, and Community Impact
**Primary Sources:** Tuskegee Experiment Station Bulletins, National Park Service Archives.

---

## 1. Deep Historical Context & Breakthroughs
- **Restoring Southern Agriculture:** Recognizing that cotton farming was destroying soil fertility and leaving farmers in perpetual debt, Carver researched and promoted alternative crops—specifically sweet potatoes and peanuts.
- **Hundreds of Derivatives:** Developed over 300 products from peanuts and 100 from sweet potatoes (including synthetic rubber, dyes, flour, and oils) to create industrial demand and economic resilience for poor farmers.
- **Accessible Education:** Published free, highly practical bulletins for farmers explaining crop rotation, soil enrichment, and food preservation in plain language.

## 2. Core Mental Models & Working Habits
- **Zero-Waste Ingenuity:** Built his laboratory apparatus out of discarded bottles, tin cans, and scrap metal, proving that lack of capital is never a valid excuse for inaction.
- **Nature as Teacher:** Began every day with early morning walks in the woods, viewing scientific inquiry as a spiritual dialogue with creation.
- **Service Before Status:** Turned down lucrative offers from Thomas Edison and industrial giants to remain at Tuskegee serving impoverished farmers.

## 3. Imitate / Adapt / Avoid
- **Imitate:** Solving practical last-mile problems with minimal resources; making advanced knowledge accessible to ordinary people.
- **Adapt:** Applying sustainable, circular-economy principles to engineering and environmental challenges in developing regions.
- **Avoid:** Pursuing research for academic vanity rather than tangible human benefit.
